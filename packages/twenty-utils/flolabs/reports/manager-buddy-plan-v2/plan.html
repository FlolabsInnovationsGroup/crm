Implementation plan v2 · FloLabs recruiting team · 31 Oct 2026

Manager Buddy

An AI teammate added next to our CRM. You talk to it by chat or voice. It acts with your permissions, remembers everything, works on its own, finds and follows up leads, warns us when something breaks, and shows what it costs, live.
Two rules for every step of this plan
1

Nothing in the existing CRM is removed or changed

Buddy is added next to the CRM, not inside it. It uses the CRM's existing features through the APIs the chatbot already uses. Existing code, screens, workflows and data model stay exactly as they are.

2

Company data is protected, and every change is tested before and after

Buddy never touches the CRM's database directly. It reads and writes only through the CRM, with the permissions of the person it acts for. Every release is tested on a copy of production data first, and the full CRM is re-tested afterwards.

03 · Access

Buddy can do what you can do, and nothing more

You said role-based access is already in the CRM, and you are right. We checked how it treats an outside app that acts for a person: the CRM allows an action only if both the person's role and the app's own role allow it. So Buddy inherits the access model without a line of new permission code.

You ask"Move these 12 people to Contacted."
→
Buddy calls the CRMwith your login token, through the chatbot's tools.
→
The CRM checksyour role, and Buddy's app role. Both must allow it.
→
Allowed: done and loggedNot allowed: refused, and Buddy tells you why.

Checked in code: resolveCallerRoles in mcp-protocol.service.ts and build-user-role-ids-by-source.util.ts ("An application acting for a user is bounded by both the user's role and its own"). The CRM's OAuth server accepts self-registered apps with authorization-code and refresh-token grants (oauth-registration.controller.ts). End-to-end login is tested on staging in phase 0.

04 · Memory

How Buddy remembers everything

Remembering everything is mostly a storage and search problem, not an AI problem. We keep a complete record in Buddy's own database, in layers, and memory is never allowed to become a way around permissions.

1. FactsStages, counts, who replied, who signed. Never copied as truth. Always read live from the CRM, with your permissions.CRM, live
2. Event logEvery conversation, voice transcript, email handled, action, alert and run. Append-only, searchable by person and date.Buddy Postgres
3. Search by meaningEmails, notes, form answers, transcripts and lead research, as vectors, plus keyword search for names and emails.pgvector + full-text
4. Knowledge graphPeople, companies, leads, team members and campaigns, joined by dated links: replied to, interested in, met, referred by, works at.Postgres graph tables
5. Team rules"Never email applicants on Fridays." Small, edited by the team, loaded every time. Plus Buddy's own notes through Claude's memory tool.Buddy Postgres
6. Working memoryThe conversation in hand. Long threads are summarised automatically (compaction) and repeated context is cached.Claude context

Options we looked at

OptionWhat it isLicenseVerdict
Postgres + pgvector + full-textVectors and keywords in one database we already know how to run and back upPostgreSQLStart here
Apache AGEGraph queries (openCypher) inside Postgres. Current releases target Postgres 17 and 18Apache-2.0If needed
Graphiti (with Neo4j)Temporal knowledge graph: old facts expire instead of being overwritten. Pre-1.0, AI cost on every writeApache-2.0 (Neo4j CE: GPLv3)Trial later
Mem0Memory layer for preferences and facts, vector and graphApache-2.0Alternative
Letta, CogneeFull agent runtime with memory tiers (Letta); graph and vector knowledge engine (Cognee)Apache-2.0Alternative
KùzuEmbedded graph databaseMIT, archived Oct 2025Avoid
FalkorDBGraph database used by some memory toolsSSPLv1, source-availableAvoid
Anthropic memory storesMemory hosted by Anthropic (Managed Agents, beta)ServiceNot now

How much storage

43k people and about 100k emails is roughly 150M tokens of text. As vectors plus text and indexes that is about 2 to 4 GB, growing 1 to 2 GB a year. Transcripts only, no audio, unless we decide otherwise.

Permissions stay intact

Each memory points to its source record. Before Buddy shows a memory to you, it re-checks that record with your login. If you can't open it in the CRM, you don't see it through Buddy.

Forgetting on request

When a person is deleted in the CRM, a webhook tells Buddy to delete their memories too (GDPR). Embeddings come from a local model by default, so text does not leave our servers.

CRM-SAFEMemory lives in Buddy's own Postgres on Buddy's server. It is filled from webhooks and from slow, throttled reads at night. It never connects to the CRM's database and never writes back to it.

05 · Tasks, autonomy and monitoring

Give it work, let it run, hear about what matters

Say it

Chat or voice: "Every Monday send me the funnel." "Remind me about Lina in 3 months."

Email it

From a team address. Orders from anyone else are ignored.

Assign it

Create a CRM task and assign it to the Buddy user, using the Tasks we already have.

Schedule it

Recurring jobs: briefing at 08:00, lead run at night, checks every 5 minutes.

The scheduler is a durable job queue in Buddy's own Postgres (pg-boss): retries, waits of months, no job runs twice, nothing is lost on restart. It never uses the CRM's Redis or worker.

How much freedom, per kind of action

LevelBuddy...Examples
DOacts and logs itRead and search, answer questions, reports, notes, its own tasks, alerts to the team
DO AND TELLacts, then tells the ownerRoutine email replies once trusted, follow-up reminders, nurture emails to old contacts
ASK FIRSTdrafts it and waits for a yesA new kind of email reply, stage changes, bulk edits, merges, deletes, new workflows
NEVERblocked in codeChange roles or settings, act beyond the user's role, decide an applicant's outcome, go over budget

Every kind of email reply starts as Ask first. It moves to Do and tell after two weeks with 90% or more of its drafts sent unchanged. You can see this score per kind of email at any time. This is how Buddy gets to replying on its own without surprising anyone.

1. Buddy app

A web app you can install on desktop and phone, with a push-to-talk button. No CRM change. First to ship.

2. Inside the CRM

A voice button as a CRM app. Our CRM already supports apps with a "Use your microphone" permission. Installed through the app system, not by editing the CRM. To confirm on our version in staging.

3. Phone

Call a number and talk to Buddy, through Telnyx. Later, once voice in the app works well.

Making it feel quick

  • Target: first words back in about a second on office internet. Measured in phase 4, not promised up front.
  • Streaming everywhere, a cached system prompt, and a "start speaking immediately" instruction to cut Claude's time to first word.
  • Quick lookups go to Claude Haiku 5.5. Anything needing several tools goes to Sonnet 5.5, with a short "checking..." while it works.
  • Language: Kokoro covers English and some other languages. If we need Arabic voice (Ahmed's forms), a hosted voice is the safer choice. To test.
  • Today, without any work: the CRM's chatbot already has dictation (speech to text in the browser).
CRM-SAFEThe voice worker runs on Buddy's server and only talks to Buddy. Option 2 uses the CRM's own app system, and nothing is installed in production until it passes the staging tests.

Guardrails for outreach

  • A separate outreach domain and mailboxes, so cold email can never hurt hr@'s delivery to applicants.
  • Warm-up, then a small daily cap per mailbox (start around 20), raised slowly.
  • Unsubscribe link in every email, a shared opt-out list, quiet hours.
  • All testing with internal +crmtest addresses first.

Legal, to check before the first send

  • GDPR: a legitimate-interest check, and telling people where we got their details within a month (Art. 14).
  • Cold email rules differ by country. Germany, for example, requires consent even for B2B.
  • Site terms: no LinkedIn scraping, its terms forbid it.

Workflows

Buddy drafts CRM workflows with the CRM's own workflow tools (list, create steps, connect, activate). Rule: anything Buddy builds is created switched off and named "[Buddy] ...". A person reviews it and switches it on. For integrations outside the CRM (Calendly, Zoom, Slack), Buddy builds n8n workflows through n8n's API, on the n8n server we already run.

Apify cost: Starter plan about $29 a month, plus about $1 to $7 per 1,000 places, depending on the actor. Third-party figures, to confirm on apify.com.

CRM-SAFELeads are added as new records only. No existing record is changed without a person's yes. Raw scraped data stays in Buddy's database, and only qualified leads go into the CRM. New workflows are created switched off and existing ones are never edited.

Manager Buddy

An AI teammate for the recruiting team that works on its own, around the clock. It follows up, finishes tasks that take days, watches for problems, and tells you what it did and what needs you. You can also talk to it like a colleague, by chat, voice or email.

What we're asking for

Three yeses to start

  2. Start the first three weeks: a safe test copy of the CRM, then Buddy's core with a live cost meter.
  3. Set the AI budget at $35 a month, as a hard cap.

Technical detail: how Buddy runs on its own
  • Work starts in four ways: schedules, CRM events by webhook (new email, form submitted, record changed, workflow run finished), tasks assigned to the Buddy user in the CRM, and messages from allowlisted team addresses.
  • A task becomes a plan stored in Buddy's database. Each step is a durable job in pg-boss on Buddy's own Postgres, with retries and a unique key, so no step runs twice and nothing is lost on a restart. A wait of days or months is just a scheduled job.
  • Before each action Buddy checks the autonomy level for that kind of action, the acting person's role (enforced by the CRM), the budget and quiet hours. Anything above its level becomes an approval.
  • Progress goes onto the CRM task as notes. When the work is done Buddy closes its own task; when it's stuck it creates a task for the owner. If the person who assigned it loses access, the task stops and the team is told.
  • Buddy watches itself too: a missed or doubled job, or spend at 50, 80 and 100% of budget, emails the admin. At the cap it pauses.

A teammate that sits next to the CRM and works through it on its own, like a new hire with their own desk who never logs off.

Works the pipeline day and night: follow-ups, reply drafts, tasks that take days, alerts when something breaks, and a morning briefing on what it did. You can also just ask it things.

The two rules

Every step of the plan keeps these two promises

1

Nothing in the existing CRM is removed or changed

Buddy is added next to the CRM, not inside it. It goes through the same doors the CRM's own chatbot uses. Switch Buddy off and the CRM works exactly as it does today.

2

Company data is protected, and every release is tested

Buddy never opens the CRM's database directly. Before each release we test on a copy of the real CRM, once with Buddy off and once with it on, and only then go live.

Technical detail: how the rules are enforced
  • Buddy is a separate service with its own server, Postgres, job queue and logs. It has no access to the CRM's database or Redis.
  • It reaches the CRM only through public interfaces: the MCP endpoint the chatbot uses (engine/api/mcp), REST and GraphQL, the CRM's OAuth server, and webhooks.
  • CRM code changes: none in every phase. If a gap ever needs one, it goes in as new files behind a switch that is off by default, tested on staging, and listed in a "CRM touchpoints" register. Today the register is empty.
  • Every release: restore the latest production backup to staging, run the CRM regression suite with Buddy off, switch Buddy on and run its own tests, run the CRM suite again including response times (within 10%), then release right after a backup. A Buddy release never ships together with a CRM release.
  • The regression suite covers login and permissions per role, People and Applications views, public forms, team inbox and email, workflows and the chatbot. It's automated with Playwright (the repo already has twenty-e2e-testing) plus a 15-minute manual checklist.
  • Staging comes from a backup because a fresh, empty install currently fails to create its first workspace (the FORM_SUBMISSIONS widget type issue). Production is not affected.
  • Rollback is stopping Buddy. Every record Buddy creates or edits is logged with who asked and why, so it can be listed and reverted.
Permissions

Buddy can do what you can do, and nothing more

Buddy logs in as the person it's helping, using the CRM's own sign-in. The CRM then checks every action against that person's role, just like it does for them. A read-only user gets a read-only Buddy.

Technical detail: we checked this in our code
  • When an app acts for a user, the CRM allows an action only if both the user's role and the app's own role allow it: resolveCallerRoles in mcp-protocol.service.ts and build-user-role-ids-by-source.util.ts.
  • The CRM's OAuth server accepts self-registered apps with the authorization-code and refresh-token grants (oauth-registration.controller.ts). Each person connects Buddy once.
  • Option A (recommended): use the existing APIs, so no CRM code changes, and the CRM is unaffected if Buddy fails. Option B, a module inside the CRM, is only for a gap the APIs can't cover.
  • API load stays under the CRM's own rate limits for apps; Buddy paces itself below them. End-to-end login is tested on staging in week 1.

Every kind of email starts at Ask first. It moves to Do and tell only after two weeks in which 90% or more of its drafts were sent unchanged. Anyone can see that score for each kind of email.

Decisions about applicants stay with people. GDPR forbids decisions about people made only by a machine, and the EU AI Act treats AI in recruitment as high-risk.

Technical detail: scheduling and checks
  • Work arrives four ways: chat or voice, email from an allowlisted team address, a CRM task assigned to the Buddy user, or a schedule.
  • The scheduler is a durable job queue in Buddy's own Postgres (pg-boss): retries, waits of months, no job runs twice, nothing lost on restart. It never uses the CRM's Redis or worker.
  • Failed workflow runs come from the workflow tools (list_workflow_runs) and webhooks; mailbox state from the sync status in the API; health from the health endpoint and response times.
  • Buddy only reads, and adds notes, tasks and emails through the CRM. It doesn't turn on, edit or delete existing CRM workflows.
How it fits together

Next to the CRM, never inside it

Technical detail: components and outside services
  • Login: CRM OAuth plus a team allowlist. Brain: a Claude agent loop with a model router (Haiku 5.5 for sorting and quick lookups, Sonnet 5.5 for conversation and drafts, Opus 5.5 only for planning).
  • Tools adapter: the only part that knows the CRM. It also wraps Apify, n8n and email. Contract tests run on every CRM upgrade.
  • Model adapter: every model call goes through one thin adapter, so FloBrain can replace Claude later as one new adapter, one job at a time, with Claude as fallback. No extra FloBrain work in this plan.
  • Reused from the CRM as is: MCP tools, OAuth, roles, webhooks, Tasks, the workflow tools, the chatbot's usage records (agentTurn), and the app system for an optional voice button or cost widget inside the CRM.
  • Outside services: Anthropic; speech (local models or Deepgram and Cartesia); Apify for lead search; n8n for Calendly, Zoom and Slack; Telnyx for phone calls later.
Memory

It remembers, but it checks the facts

Buddy keeps a record of conversations, emails, people, leads and decisions, so nobody has to explain things twice. You can search it by meaning: "who mentioned robotics experience?"

For facts like stages, counts and who replied, it never trusts its memory. It reads them live from the CRM, so it can't report something that's no longer true. And it won't show you a memory from a record you can't open in the CRM.

Technical detail: six layers, all in Buddy's own Postgres
  1. Facts read live from the CRM with the user's permissions, never copied as truth.
  2. Event log, append-only: every conversation, transcript, email, action, alert and run.
  3. Search by meaning: pgvector plus full-text search for names and addresses. Embeddings from a local model by default, so text stays on our servers.
  4. Knowledge graph in Postgres tables: people, companies, leads and campaigns with dated links (replied to, met, referred by).
  5. Team rules, small and edited by the team, loaded every time ("never email applicants on Fridays").
  6. Working memory: the conversation in hand, compacted when long, repeated context cached.
  • Each memory points to its source record and is re-checked with the user's login before it's shown.
  • When a person is deleted in the CRM, a webhook deletes their memories too (GDPR).
  • About 2 to 4 GB for 43k people and about 100k emails, growing 1 to 2 GB a year. Filled from webhooks and slow, throttled reads at night.
  • Start with Postgres. Graphiti or Mem0 are options to trial later. Avoid Kùzu (archived October 2025) and FalkorDB (SSPL licence).
Voice and leads

Talk to it, and let it find new contacts

Voice. Hold a button and talk, then Buddy answers out loud. It's the same Buddy as in chat, with the same permissions and memory. It comes first in the Buddy web app, then optionally as a button inside the CRM, and later as a phone number. Free local voices cover English. If we need Arabic, we'll use a paid voice.

Leads (weeks 9 to 12). Buddy searches for new contacts, removes anyone already in the CRM, scores the rest, and adds only qualified ones. Then it sends a personal first email and a few follow-ups, and tells the owner within five minutes when someone is interested. Cold email goes from a separate domain, with small daily limits and an unsubscribe link, so it can never hurt delivery of our emails to applicants.

Technical detail: voice pipeline and lead flow
  • Anthropic has no public real-time voice API, so voice is a pipeline: microphone, turn detection, speech-to-text, Buddy's brain, text-to-speech. Target: first words back in about a second, measured in phase 4.
  • Recommended: Pipecat (BSD-2) for the pipeline; Moonshine or faster-whisper for speech-to-text; Kokoro (Apache-2.0, CPU) for the voice. Hosted options: Deepgram Nova-3, Cartesia Sonic or ElevenLabs Flash. Phone via Telnyx later.
  • The CRM's app system supports apps with a microphone permission, so a voice button inside the CRM needs no CRM change. To confirm on our version in staging.
  • Leads: Apify actors through Apify's official MCP server, dedupe by email and domain, rules first then Haiku 5.5 through the Batch API for scoring, Sonnet 5.5 for first emails. Raw scraped data stays in Buddy's database.
  • Before the first send: a GDPR legitimate-interest check and telling people where we got their details (Art. 14); country rules (Germany needs consent even for B2B); no LinkedIn scraping.
  • Workflows Buddy drafts are created switched off and named "[Buddy] ...". A person reviews and switches them on. Existing workflows are never edited.

Questions people ask

Straight answers

What does it do when nobody is around?
It keeps working: it sorts new email, sends follow-ups on time, carries on with tasks it was given, retries or reports anything that failed, and prepares the next morning's briefing. Anything it isn't trusted to do alone waits for a person.
What stops it going wrong at night, with nobody watching?
The same limits apply day and night. It acts only within people's roles, new kinds of email wait for approval, it keeps quiet hours, and spending has a daily cap. Every action is logged and can be listed and reverted, and one switch pauses it.
Will it change or slow down the CRM?
No. It runs on its own server and uses the CRM the way any outside app would, within the CRM's own limits. Response times are part of every release test, and must stay within 10%.
Can it show me things I'm not allowed to see?
No. It acts with your role, and the CRM checks every request. Memories are re-checked against the CRM with your login before you see them.
Will it email applicants without asking?
Not at first. Every kind of email starts as a draft that a person approves. A kind of email may go out on its own only after two weeks in which 90% of its drafts were sent unchanged, and the team can see that score.
What if an email tries to trick it?
Text from applicants and leads is treated as information, never as instructions. Only team members can give Buddy orders, and what it may do is fixed by the autonomy levels, whatever an email says.
What if it breaks, or costs too much?
We switch it off and the CRM carries on as before. Spending is capped per day, per person and per month, with alerts at 50, 80 and 100%, and Buddy pauses at the cap.
Is this legal under GDPR?
Buddy never decides on an applicant, it keeps a log of every action, deleting a person in the CRM deletes their memories, and lead outreach gets a legitimate-interest check and country rules before the first send. A DPIA comes before anything ranks applicants. This is a summary, not legal advice.
